\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document sets out, in a form directly translatable into a paper algorithm, the
constrained-committee method used for \texttt{Al\_12\_4\_6A\_2\_}: how a linear ACE
potential's parameter vector is fitted under physical constraints, how a POPS
uncertainty committee is generated from it, and --- the substantive addition --- how
dynamical-stability constraints are imposed at \emph{several lattice constants} rather
than one. Every equation is stated in the form actually implemented, and every result is
attributed to the script and output directory that produced it. Section~\ref{sec:map}
is the script/result map; Section~\ref{sec:pitfalls} records four failure modes that
were diagnosed along the way and which any write-up should mention, because three of
them silently produced plausible-looking but wrong numbers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and the one-sentence summary}

A linear ACE potential is fitted by a quadratic program (QP) whose inequality rows
encode physics: elastic (Born) stability, a pinned equilibrium lattice constant, and
positive phonon frequencies throughout the Brillouin zone. A POPS committee is then
drawn by rejection sampling under the same constraints. The finding this document
exists to support is:

\begin{quote}
Imposing phonon stability at the equilibrium lattice constant \emph{alone} is
insufficient --- constrained members are stable at $\aeq$ yet leave the FCC lattice
within picoseconds under NPT molecular dynamics. Imposing it on a grid of lattice
constants spanning the thermal-expansion range fixes this, and yields a physically
meaningful thermal-expansion coefficient.
\end{quote}

%=====================================================================
\section{Notation and the linearity that makes everything work}
\label{sec:notation}

\subsection{Linear ACE}

For a configuration $R$ of atomic positions, the ACE energy is \emph{linear} in the
parameter vector $\bm\theta\in\R^{K}$ ($K=91$ here):
\begin{equation}
E(R;\bm\theta) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{K}\theta_k\,B_k(R) \;=\; \bm B(R)\cdot\bm\theta ,
\label{eq:ace}
\end{equation}
where $B_k$ are the ACE basis functions. This is the single structural fact the entire
method rests on: because differentiation is linear, \emph{every} derived quantity that
is a linear or bilinear functional of $E$ inherits an exact affine dependence on
$\bm\theta$. In particular the force-constant (Hessian) matrix
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{a\alpha,b\beta}(\bm\theta)
=\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial r_{a\alpha}\,\partial r_{b\beta}}
=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\theta_k\,\underbrace{\frac{\partial^2 B_k}{\partial r_{a\alpha}\partial r_{b\beta}}}_{\textstyle \Phi^{(k)}_{a\alpha,b\beta}}
\label{eq:undotted}
\end{equation}
splits into $K$ geometry-only tensors $\Phi^{(k)}$. We call $\{\Phi^{(k)}\}$ the
\emph{undotted Hessian}: it depends on the geometry but not on $\bm\theta$, so it is
built once per geometry and reused for every committee member. That is what makes a
full-Brillouin-zone phonon check cheap enough to serve as a rejection-sampling
predicate.

Implementation: \file{scripts/bandpath_phonon_uq/lib.jl}, functions
\jl{_site_basis_hessian} (nested \texttt{ForwardDiff} of \jl{evaluate_basis} at site
level) and \jl{undotted_hessian} (threaded accumulation over atoms, with the four
$\pm$ blocks per neighbour pair that enforce translational invariance).

\subsection{Design system and preconditioner}

Let $\bm A\in\R^{M\times K}$ be the design matrix (row $m$ = basis evaluation for
observation $m$), $\bm y\in\R^{M}$ the observations (energies/forces/virials), $\bm W$
the diagonal weight matrix, and $\bm P$ the diagonal preconditioner supplied by the
fitting pipeline. Define
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bm A} \;=\; \bm W\bm A\bm P^{-1},\qquad
\tilde{\bm y} \;=\; \bm W\bm y,\qquad
\tilde{\bm\theta} \;=\; \bm P\bm\theta ,\qquad
\lambda = 1/M .
\label{eq:precond}
\end{equation}
All optimisation is carried out in the preconditioned variable $\tilde{\bm\theta}$ and
mapped back by $\bm\theta=\bm P^{-1}\tilde{\bm\theta}$.

\section{The constrained fit}
\label{sec:qp}

\subsection{Objective}

In OSQP's convention $\min_{\bm x}\ \tfrac12\bm x^{\!\top}\bm P_{\!\mathrm{qp}}\bm x+\bm q^{\!\top}\bm x$
subject to $\bm l\le \bm A_{\mathrm{qp}}\bm x\le \bm u$, the code sets
\begin{equation}
\bm P_{\!\mathrm{qp}} = \tilde{\bm A}^{\!\top}\tilde{\bm A} + \lambda\,\bm P^{\!\top}\bm P,
\qquad
\bm q = -\tilde{\bm A}^{\!\top}\tilde{\bm y},
\end{equation}
so that, up to an additive constant, the objective is
\begin{equation}
J(\tilde{\bm\theta})
= \tfrac12\big\|\tilde{\bm A}\tilde{\bm\theta}-\tilde{\bm y}\big\|_2^{2}
\;+\;\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\big\|\bm P\tilde{\bm\theta}\big\|_2^{2}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A subtlety worth stating explicitly in the paper.}
The regulariser is written in the \emph{preconditioned} variable. Substituting
$\tilde{\bm\theta}=\bm P\bm\theta$ gives
$\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\bm P^{2}\bm\theta\|^{2}$, i.e.\ the effective Tikhonov prior in
coefficient space is
\begin{equation}
\bm\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}} \;=\; \bm P^{2},
\label{eq:gammaeff}
\end{equation}
not $\bm P$. This is a property of the implementation, not an accident of notation; if
the paper reports the prior it should report $\bm P^{2}$.

\subsection{Transporting constraint rows}

A physical constraint is naturally written as a row $\bm g$ acting on the coefficients,
$\bm g\cdot\bm\theta \ge c$. In the solver variable this becomes
\begin{equation}
\bm g\cdot\bm\theta \;=\; \bm g\,\bm P^{-1}\tilde{\bm\theta},
\end{equation}
which is why every constraint row appears in the code as \jl{row/P} (right division by
the diagonal $\bm P$) and the solution is recovered as \jl{P \ r.x}.

%=====================================================================
\section{The POPS committee}
\label{sec:pops}

\subsection{Pointwise corrections (the ``naive'' forest)}

Write
\begin{equation}
\bm C = \tilde{\bm A}^{\!\top}\tilde{\bm A}+\lambda\bm P^{2},\qquad
\bm G = \bm C^{-1}\tilde{\bm A}^{\!\top},\qquad
h_i = \big(\tilde{\bm A}\bm G\big)_{ii},\qquad
\bm r = \tilde{\bm y}-\tilde{\bm A}\tilde{\bm\theta}_{\mathrm{LSQ}} ,
\label{eq:levres}
\end{equation}
where $h_i$ is the leverage of observation $i$ and $r_i$ its residual. The POPS
pointwise correction and the corresponding committee member are
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\delta\bm\theta^{(i)} \;=\; \bm P^{-1}\,\bm G_{:,i}\,\frac{r_i}{h_i},
\qquad
\bm\theta^{(i)} \;=\; \bm\theta_{\mathrm{LSQ}} + \delta\bm\theta^{(i)} . \;}
\label{eq:pops}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Interpretation (this is the cleanest way to motivate POPS in the paper).}
The correction is exactly the perturbation that makes the model \emph{interpolate}
observation $i$. Indeed
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bm A}_{i,:}\big(\bm P\,\delta\bm\theta^{(i)}\big)
=\tilde{\bm A}_{i,:}\bm G_{:,i}\,\frac{r_i}{h_i}
= h_i\,\frac{r_i}{h_i}
= r_i ,
\label{eq:interp}
\end{equation}
so the member's prediction at $i$ moves by precisely the residual there, closing it.
Each committee member is therefore ``the fit that would have been obtained had
observation $i$ been reproduced exactly''; the ensemble spread measures how far the fit
can be pushed by individual data.

Members are selected as the $n_{\mathrm{lev}}=5$ highest-leverage observations, the
$n_{\mathrm{res}}=10$ largest-$|r|$ observations not already chosen, and
$n_{\mathrm{rand}}=15$ random others, giving $N=30$ members
(\jl{Random.seed!(1234)} makes this reproducible).

\subsection{Constrained members}

The constrained analogue replaces \eqref{eq:pops} by a QP that imposes the
interpolation condition \eqref{eq:interp} as an \emph{equality row} while adding the
physics rows of Section~\ref{sec:constraints}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bm\theta^{(i)}_{\mathrm{con}} = \bm P^{-1}\ \argmin_{\tilde{\bm\theta}}\quad & J(\tilde{\bm\theta})\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& \tilde{\bm A}_{i,:}\,\tilde{\bm\theta} = \tilde y_i && \text{(interpolate obs.\ $i$)}\\
& \big(\bm b'\bm P^{-1}\big)\tilde{\bm\theta} = 0 && \text{(equilibrium at $\aeq$)}\\
& \bm l_{\mathrm{phys}} \le \bm G_{\mathrm{phys}}\bm P^{-1}\tilde{\bm\theta} \le \bm\infty . &&
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:conqp}
\end{equation}
The constrained \emph{mean} model $\bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}}$ is the same program with
the interpolation row removed.

%=====================================================================
\section{The physical constraint rows}
\label{sec:constraints}

\subsection{Equation of state: pinning the equilibrium}

Let $\bm b(a)=\bm B\big(\text{reference cell at lattice constant }a\big)$, so that the
per-cell energy is $E(a;\bm\theta)=\bm b(a)\cdot\bm\theta$ --- again linear in
$\bm\theta$. With $\bm b'=\dd\bm b/\dd a|_{\aeq}$ and
$\bm b''=\dd^{2}\bm b/\dd a^{2}|_{\aeq}$ (both by \texttt{ForwardDiff}):
\begin{equation}
\bm b'\cdot\bm\theta = 0 \quad\text{(equality)},
\qquad
\bm b''\cdot\bm\theta \ge \varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon=10^{-9}.
\label{eq:eos}
\end{equation}
The first states that $\aeq$ is a stationary point of the energy--volume curve; the
second that it is a minimum. Together they pin every committee member's equilibrium
lattice constant to the common reference $\aeq$, which is what makes a \emph{fixed}
reference geometry legitimate for the phonon rows.

\subsection{Born elastic stability}

The Voigt elastic operator is again linear in $\bm\theta$:
\begin{equation}
C_{ij}(\bm\theta)=\sum_{k}\theta_k H^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij,k},
\qquad
H^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij,k}=\frac{\partial^{2}B_k}{\partial\varepsilon_i\,\partial\varepsilon_j}\bigg|_{\bm\varepsilon=0},
\end{equation}
computed by \jl{elastic_hessian_basis} in \file{src/Elasticity/get_hessian.jl}. The
three cubic Born criteria are imposed as rows
\begin{equation}
C_{44}\ge 0.1,\qquad C_{11}-C_{12}\ge 1.0,\qquad C_{11}+2C_{12}\ge 0.1 ,
\label{eq:born}
\end{equation}
in the raw (eV, per reference cell) units of $H^{\mathrm{el}}$; conversion to GPa uses
$160.2176621/|\det \bm L_0|$ and is applied only for reporting. Stacking with
\eqref{eq:eos} gives the matrix \jl{born_rows} with lower bounds
\jl{born_lower = [0.1, 1.0, 0.1, 1e-9]}.

\subsection{Phonons: the dynamical matrix is affine in \texorpdfstring{$\bm\theta$}{theta}}

From the undotted Hessian \eqref{eq:undotted}, the Bloch transform to the primitive-cell
dynamical matrix is a fixed linear map, so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\bm D(\bm q;\bm\theta) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{K}\theta_k\,\bm D^{(k)}(\bm q)\;}
\label{eq:Dlinear}
\end{equation}
with, for primitive-cell atoms $i,j$ and supercell atoms $l$,
\begin{equation}
D^{(k)}_{i\alpha,j\beta}(\bm q)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_i m_j}}
\sum_{\substack{l\,:\,\mathrm{s2p}(l)=j}}
\Phi^{(k)}_{\,\mathrm{p2s}(i)\alpha,\;l\beta}\;
e^{\,\mathrm{i}\,\bm q\cdot\bm R_l},
\qquad
\bm R_l = \mathrm{MIC}\big(\bm L(\bm f_l-\bm f_j)\big),
\label{eq:bloch}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{MIC}$ is the minimum-image convention applied in \emph{supercell}
fractional coordinates (wrapping to $(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$), and $\bm D$ is
Hermitised afterwards. Implementation: \jl{precompute_force_constants} and
\jl{dynamical_matrix_from_fc} in \file{src/Phonons/phonon_bands.jl}; the per-basis
assembly into $\{\bm D^{(k)}\}$ over the whole band path is \jl{bandpath_Dk} in
\file{scripts/bandpath_phonon_uq/lib.jl}.

Frequencies follow from the eigenvalues $\mu_\nu$ of $\bm D$:
\begin{equation}
\omega_\nu(\bm q;\bm\theta) \;=\; \sgn(\mu_\nu)\sqrt{|\mu_\nu|}\;\cdot f,
\qquad
f=\frac{1}{2\pi\times10^{12}}\sqrt{\frac{e}{\text{\AA}^{2}\,u}}\approx 15.633\ \mathrm{THz},
\label{eq:freq}
\end{equation}
the signed square root being the standard device for displaying imaginary (unstable)
modes as negative frequencies.

\paragraph{Stability metric.} Acoustic branches vanish at $\Gamma$, so the scalar used
throughout is the minimum \emph{non-acoustic} frequency over the sampled path:
\begin{equation}
m(\bm\theta) \;=\; \min_{\bm q\,:\,\|\bm q\|\ge q_\Gamma}\ \min_{\nu}\ \omega_\nu(\bm q;\bm\theta),
\qquad q_\Gamma = 5\times10^{-2},
\label{eq:minfreq}
\end{equation}
implemented as \jl{min_freq_stable}. The path is
$\Gamma\!\to\!X\!\to\!U\!\to\!L\!\to\!\Gamma\!\to\!K$ with per-segment sampling
\jl{N_per_seg = [20,20,20,20,60]} --- the final $\Gamma\!\to\!K$ leg is sampled
three times more densely because the soft modes concentrate there and a sharp dip
between $q$-points would otherwise escape both the constraint and the check. Per-segment
sampling required extending \jl{fcc_band_path} in \file{src/Phonons/phonon_bands.jl} to
accept a vector.

\section{Cutting-plane repair}
\label{sec:cuts}

Requiring $\min_\nu\mu_\nu(\bm q;\bm\theta)\ge\omega_{\mathrm{cut}}^{2}$ is not a linear
constraint on $\bm\theta$. It is, however, the infimum of linear constraints. For any
fixed unit vector $\bm e$, the Rayleigh quotient
\begin{equation}
\bm e^{\dagger}\bm D(\bm q;\bm\theta)\,\bm e
\;=\;\sum_{k}\theta_k\,\bm e^{\dagger}\bm D^{(k)}(\bm q)\,\bm e
\;=\;\bm g(\bm q,\bm e)\cdot\bm\theta,
\qquad
g_k(\bm q,\bm e) = \Real\!\big[\bm e^{\dagger}\bm D^{(k)}(\bm q)\bm e\big]
\label{eq:cutrow}
\end{equation}
is an \emph{exact linear functional} of $\bm\theta$ --- no finite differences, no
displacement amplitude. This is \jl{cut_row(iq,e,bp)}, implemented as
$\mathrm{vec}(\bar{\bm e}\bm e^{\!\top})^{\!\top}\bm B_q$, which reproduces
$\bm e^{\dagger}\bm D^{(k)}\bm e$ exactly under column-major \texttt{vec}.

Because $\bm e^{\dagger}\bm D\bm e\ \ge\ \mu_{\min}(\bm D)$ for every unit $\bm e$, a
single row \eqref{eq:cutrow} is \emph{necessary but not sufficient}: enforcing it at the
current softest eigenvector does not guarantee the new $\bm\theta$ has
$\mu_{\min}\ge\omega_{\mathrm{cut}}^{2}$, because the eigenvector rotates as
$\bm\theta$ moves. The remedy is the standard cutting-plane loop: re-diagonalise, add
rows for whatever is still soft, re-solve, iterate. Convergence is declared when no soft
mode remains; \jl{max_cuts = 40} caps the iterations.

\begin{equation}
\omega^{2}_{\mathrm{cut}} = \left(\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{THz}}_{\mathrm{cut}}}{f}\right)^{2},
\qquad \omega^{\mathrm{THz}}_{\mathrm{cut}} = 0.15\ \mathrm{THz}.
\end{equation}

Note the asymmetry between the two stages, which the paper should state: the
\emph{repair} stage imposes $\bm b'\cdot\bm\theta=0$ exactly (an equality,
$l=u=0$), whereas the \emph{rejection} stage cannot --- an equality has zero acceptance
probability under a continuous proposal --- and instead imposes the band
\eqref{eq:band} below.

\section{Hypercube proposal and rejection sampling}
\label{sec:sampling}

Collect the $N$ constrained corrections as rows of
$\bm\Delta\in\R^{N\times K}$, $\bm\Delta_{i,:}=\bm\theta^{(i)}_{\mathrm{con}}-\bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}}$.
Diagonalise the second-moment matrix and keep the numerically supported directions,
\begin{equation}
\bm\Delta^{\!\top}\bm\Delta = \bm V\bm\Lambda\bm V^{\!\top},
\qquad
\text{keep }j:\ \Lambda_{jj} > 10^{-8}\max_l\Lambda_{ll},
\end{equation}
project the corrections onto the retained eigenbasis, $\bm\Pi=\bm\Delta\bm V$, and take
the axis-aligned bounding box
\begin{equation}
\ell_j=\min_i \Pi_{ij},\qquad u_j=\max_i\Pi_{ij}
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
(the \jl{percentile_clipping = 0} case of \jl{hypercube}, i.e.\ the \emph{unclipped}
box: the feasible-set geometry is imposed exactly by rejection rather than approximated
by shrinking the proposal). Proposals are
\begin{equation}
\bm u\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]^{d},\qquad
\bm\theta = \bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}} + \bm V\big(\bm\ell + (\bm u_{\!\mathrm{b}}-\bm\ell)\odot\bm u\big),
\end{equation}
and are accepted iff all of the following hold (\jl{rejection_sample_hypercube} with a
predicate, \file{src/POPS/POPSRegression.jl}):
\begin{align}
&\text{(i) Born rows \eqref{eq:born} satisfied;}\notag\\[2pt]
&\text{(ii)}\quad
\frac{\big|\bm b'\cdot(\bm\theta-\bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}})\big|}{\bm b''\cdot\bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}}}\ \le\ 0.1;
\label{eq:band}\\[2pt]
&\text{(iii)}\quad m_v(\bm\theta)\ \ge\ \omega^{\mathrm{THz}}_{\mathrm{cut}}-10^{-6}
\quad\text{for every volume }v. \notag
\end{align}

\paragraph{Reading condition \eqref{eq:band}.} To first order the shift in a member's
equilibrium lattice constant is
$\Delta \aeq \simeq -\,(\dd E/\dd a)/(\dd^{2}E/\dd a^{2})
= -\,\bm b'\!\cdot\!\bm\theta\,/\,\bm b''\!\cdot\!\bm\theta$.
Condition \eqref{eq:band} is therefore, to leading order, the statement
$|\Delta \aeq|\le 0.1\,$\AA: a sampled member's equilibrium may drift by at most
$0.1$\,\AA{} from the reference. This is the continuous relaxation of the equality
$\bm b'\cdot\bm\theta=0$ used in the repair stage.

\section{The multi-volume extension}
\label{sec:multivol}

Everything above is stated at a single reference geometry. The extension is to impose
condition (iii) on a grid of lattice constants:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
a_v = s_v\,\aeq,\qquad
s_v\in\{1.00,\,1.02,\,1.04,\,1.06,\,1.08,\,1.10\},\qquad v=1,\dots,6 .\;}
\label{eq:volgrid}
\end{equation}
Because $\Phi^{(k)}$ depends on geometry, each $a_v$ requires its \emph{own} undotted
Hessian build and its own $\{\bm D^{(k,v)}(\bm q)\}$; these are cached to disk as
\file{results/undotted_Hbasis_4x4x4_a<a>.jls} ($\approx 429$\,MB each at
$4\times4\times4$, $\approx$ 1--2\,min to build on 20 threads).

Three points of design, all of which the paper should state:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{$\aeq$ remains the reference geometry.} The equation-of-state rows
\eqref{eq:eos} and the Born rows \eqref{eq:born} are still evaluated at $\aeq$ only. The
extra volumes add stability requirements; they do not move the equilibrium.
\item \textbf{The cutting plane pools volumes.} Each iteration collects soft modes from
\emph{all} $v$ and appends one row \eqref{eq:cutrow} per triple $(v,\bm q,\nu)$. In
practice this produced a median of $864$ and a maximum of $1605$ cut rows per member.
\item \textbf{The predicate short-circuits, softest volume first.} The volumes are
tested in ascending order of $m_v(\bm\theta_{\mathrm{mean}})$, so most rejections are
paid for at one volume rather than six.
\end{enumerate}

The constrained fit for member $i$ therefore becomes the program
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\tilde{\bm\theta}}\quad & J(\tilde{\bm\theta})\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& \tilde{\bm A}_{i,:}\tilde{\bm\theta}=\tilde y_i,\qquad
  \bm b'\bm P^{-1}\tilde{\bm\theta}=0,\\
& C_{44}\ge0.1,\quad C_{11}-C_{12}\ge1.0,\quad C_{11}+2C_{12}\ge0.1,\quad \bm b''\!\cdot\!\bm\theta\ge\varepsilon,\\
& \bm g(\bm q,\bm e)\,\bm P^{-1}\tilde{\bm\theta}\ \ge\ \omega_{\mathrm{cut}}^{2}
  \qquad \forall\,(v,\bm q,\bm e)\in\mathcal{S}^{(t)} ,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fullqp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}^{(t)}$ is the accumulated cut set after $t$ iterations.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Volume-resolved constrained POPS committee}
\begin{algsteps}
\item \textbf{Reference geometry.} Relax $\aeq$ from the mean model. Build $\bm b'$,
      $\bm b''$, $H^{\mathrm{el}}$ at $\aeq$; assemble \eqref{eq:eos}, \eqref{eq:born}.
\item \textbf{Volume grid.} For $v=1..6$ with $a_v$ from \eqref{eq:volgrid}: build the
      undotted Hessian $\{\Phi^{(k,v)}\}$ and the band-path operators
      $\{\bm D^{(k,v)}(\bm q)\}$. Cache to disk.
\item \textbf{Constrained mean.} Solve \eqref{eq:fullqp} without the interpolation row;
      loop: collect soft modes over all $v$, append cut rows, re-solve, until none
      remain or \jl{max_cuts}.
\item \textbf{Proposal cloud.} Select $N=30$ observations (leverage / residual /
      random). For each, solve \eqref{eq:fullqp} with its interpolation row and run the
      same multi-volume cutting-plane loop.
\item \textbf{Proposal box.} Form $\bm\Delta$ from the repaired members, diagonalise,
      take the unclipped bounding box \eqref{eq:box}.
\item \textbf{Rejection sample.} Draw uniformly in the box; accept iff Born
      \eqref{eq:born}, band \eqref{eq:band}, and $m_v\ge\omega_{\mathrm{cut}}$ for
      \emph{all} $v$ (softest first). Repeat to $N$ members.
\item \textbf{Verify and export.} Recompute $m_v$ for every member at every volume;
      write the member $\times$ volume table.
\end{algsteps}
\end{algorithm}

\section{NPT validation and the FCC-survival test}
\label{sec:npt}

A committee member is validated by NPT molecular dynamics at zero pressure (Langevin
thermostat + Monte-Carlo barostat, Molly.jl), $4\times4\times4$ FCC supercell
($256$ atoms), $T\in\{300,500,700,900\}$\,K, $10^{4}$ equilibration $+\ 2\times10^{4}$
production steps at $\Delta t=1$\,fs.

\subsection{Lattice constant and expansion coefficient}

With per-frame volumes $V_f$ over production frames $\mathcal{P}$ and $N_{\mathrm{sup}}=4$,
\begin{equation}
a(T)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{V_f^{1/3}}{N_{\mathrm{sup}}},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{1}{a_0}\frac{\dd a}{\dd T}\bigg|_{\mathrm{lin.fit}} .
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
Because the box volume changes under NPT, the RDF, MSD and coordination analyses all use
each frame's \emph{own} box for the minimum-image convention --- using a fixed box is a
silent error under a barostat.

\subsection{The FCC-survival criterion}

The essential guard. Define the mean coordination within a cutoff $r_c=3.3$\,\AA,
\begin{equation}
\langle Z\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big|\{\,j\ne i:\ r_{ij}<r_c\,\}\Big| ,
\qquad
\text{FCC declared iff }\ \langle Z\rangle\ \ge\ 12-\tau,\quad \tau=1 ,
\label{eq:fcc}
\end{equation}
alongside the median nearest-neighbour distance, whose FCC value is $a/\sqrt2$. Perfect
FCC gives $\langle Z\rangle=12$; thermal disorder at 700\,K gives $11.5$; the
transformed phase observed here gives $\approx 9.1$.

\paragraph{Why the \emph{mean} and not the maximum.} An earlier version of the analysis
logged only $\max_i Z_i$, which remains pinned at $12$ long after the lattice has
transformed, and consequently reported ``$6.3\%$ thermal expansion'' for a cell that was
no longer FCC. $\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:alpha} is fitted \emph{only} over temperatures
satisfying \eqref{eq:fcc}; temperatures that fail are plotted but excluded.

\paragraph{The 900\,K failure is of a different kind.}
At $900$\,K the lattice leaves FCC, but $a(900\,\mathrm{K})=4.3121$\,\AA{} $=1.066\,\aeq$
lies \emph{inside} the constrained grid \eqref{eq:volgrid}, and the phonon spectrum
there is \emph{positive}, $m=+0.208$\,THz. The transition therefore occurs at a geometry
where the model is harmonically stable: it is a finite-amplitude, thermally activated
transition to a competing minimum, not a dynamical instability. Harmonic constraints of
the form \eqref{eq:cutrow} cannot address it by construction. ($900$\,K is also $96\%$
of aluminium's melting point, $933$\,K.)

\paragraph{The natural next constraint.} Because $E$ is linear in $\bm\theta$
\eqref{eq:ace}, the requirement that FCC lie below a specific competing structure
$R_{\mathrm{c}}$ is exactly \emph{one} linear row,
\begin{equation}
\big(\bm B(R_{\mathrm{c}})-\bm B(R_{\mathrm{FCC}})\big)\cdot\bm\theta \;>\; 0 ,
\label{eq:competing}
\end{equation}
per atom, requiring only two basis evaluations and no Hessian. A relaxed frame of the
competing phase is available in
\file{npt_multivolume_softest/T900K/md_trajectory.extxyz}. This is untested.

\section{Four pitfalls that produced plausible but wrong numbers}
\label{sec:pitfalls}

Each of these was diagnosed during development and each, while active, produced output
that looked reasonable. They are worth a methods paragraph.

\paragraph{(a) Supercell too small for the cutoff.}
With a $6$\,\AA{} model cutoff, a $3\times3\times3$ FCC supercell has box half-width
$\approx6.07$\,\AA{} $\approx$ the cutoff, so an atom interacts with its own periodic
image and zone-boundary phonons are aliased. Converged values require
$N_{\mathrm{cell}}\ge4$. The effect is large and not a small correction: one member gave
$+0.150$\,THz at $N_{\mathrm{cell}}=3$ and $-5.199$\,THz at $N_{\mathrm{cell}}=4$ and
$5$. \emph{All} phonon numbers computed at $N_{\mathrm{cell}}=3$ are unreliable,
including those in \file{results/bandpath_undotted/}. Diagnostic:
\file{scripts/uq/phonon_pipeline_ncell_diagnosis_Al_12_4_6A_2.jl}. A cheap guard is to
assert $N_{\mathrm{cell}}\,a/2 >$ cutoff.

\paragraph{(b) Fixed-geometry checks applied to unpinned vectors.}
The band-path operators $\{\bm D^{(k)}\}$ are built at one geometry. This is valid for
constrained members precisely because $\bm b'\cdot\bm\theta=0$ pins their equilibrium
there --- but \emph{not} for naive POPS vectors, whose own relaxed $a$ differs. Naive
members must be checked with a Hessian built at their own relaxed lattice constant
(\file{scripts/uq/check_worst_naive_native4_Al_12_4_6A_2.jl}).

\paragraph{(c) Maximum coordination as a crystallinity test.}
$\max_i Z_i$ stays at $12$ in a transformed cell; only $\langle Z\rangle$ falls. Using
the maximum caused a transformation ($\langle Z\rangle:12\to9.5$, NN
$2.86\to2.58$\,\AA, $\Delta E\approx-0.18$\,eV/atom) to be reported as thermal
expansion. See Eq.~\eqref{eq:fcc}.

\paragraph{(d) Fitting $\alpha$ across a phase change.}
Because $\alpha$ is a slope, a constant offset from a transformation cancels out of it,
so a fit over transformed points can look plausible. The single-volume run's
$\alpha=18.95\times10^{-6}$ was of this kind: superficially close to experiment, but
fitted entirely on non-FCC data and unstable to refitting (leave-one-out
$[14.8,\,31.7]$). Quote leave-one-out ranges, and fit only FCC points.

\paragraph{Two open items.}
(i) Test-set coverage for the multi-volume committee was $48.1\%$ (energy RMSE
$0.972$\,eV); the corresponding single-volume figure was not retained, so whether the
tighter constraints cost calibration is \emph{not yet established}.
(ii) The committee sits on the constraint boundary --- the softest member has
$\min_v m_v = +0.1503$\,THz against $\omega^{\mathrm{THz}}_{\mathrm{cut}}=0.15$, and the
median member $+0.1586$ --- so \jl{:softest} and \jl{:median} are effectively the same
vector. Members with genuine headroom require a larger $\omega_{\mathrm{cut}}$.
