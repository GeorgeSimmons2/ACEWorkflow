\begin{abstract}
% MLST abstracts are typically 150--250 words. A reliable four-sentence
% template: (1) problem, (2) approach, (3) headline results, (4) implication.
Machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) achieve near--first-principles accuracy at a fraction of the cost, but their reliability degrades sharply in regions of configuration space that are sparsely represented in the training data. The conventional remedy---generating additional density functional theory (DFT) reference data through active learning or fine-tuning---is computationally expensive and inherits known deficiencies of DFT itself, such as poorly converged stresses. Here we introduce a framework for embedding physics-informed priors directly into the training of linear MLIPs by recasting the fit as a quadratic program with linear constraints. We demonstrate that these constraints (i) restore repulsive core behaviour without recourse to empirical baseline potentials such as ZBL, enabling stable simulation at high pressures, (ii) can encode experimentally measured properties, circumventing DFT convergence issues, and (iii) incur negligible cost in accuracy or evaluation time relative to unconstrained fits. We further couple the constrained models with uncertainty quantification via \todo{expand POPS acronym} (POPS) committees and analyse how constraints reshape predictive uncertainty inside and outside the training distribution. \todo{One sentence of headline quantitative results, e.g. RMSE numbers or pressure range accessed.} Our approach offers a cheap, data-efficient alternative to brute-force dataset expansion for patching holes in the potential energy surface.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% --- Paragraph 1: context. Why MLIPs matter. ---
Machine-learned interatomic potentials have become a standard tool for atomistic simulation, offering near-DFT accuracy at orders of magnitude lower cost \todo{cite: ACE (Drautz 2019), GAP, MTP, NequIP/MACE review}. Linear models built on systematic basis expansions, such as the atomic cluster expansion (ACE), are particularly attractive because of their fast evaluation, systematic convergence, and amenability to well-understood regression techniques \todo{cite: Drautz 2019, Lysogorskiy 2021 (PACE), Witt 2023 (ACEpotentials.jl)}.

% --- Paragraph 2: the problem. Holes in the PES. ---
A persistent failure mode of MLIPs is unphysical behaviour in regions of configuration space poorly covered by training data. The dominant strategy for addressing these ``holes'' in the potential energy surface (PES) is to generate more reference data---via active learning, hyper-active learning, or fine-tuning of foundation models \todo{cite: hyperactive learning (van der Oord 2023), D-optimality/MaxVol active learning (Podryabinkin \& Shapeev), fine-tuning papers}. This is computationally expensive, and, more fundamentally, it presumes that DFT is a trustworthy oracle everywhere. In practice DFT stresses are often poorly converged with respect to plane-wave cutoff and k-point sampling, so elastic properties inherited from DFT reference data can be systematically wrong \todo{cite: DFT stress convergence studies; possibly delta-project style benchmarks}.

% --- Paragraph 3: a concrete symptom. Non-repulsive cores. ---
A striking example is the behaviour of fitted potentials at short interatomic separations. Because typical training sets contain few or no configurations with close atomic approaches (see the pair-distance histogram in Figure~\ref{fig:eos_hist}), unconstrained models frequently exhibit spurious attractive wells inside the repulsive core region. Such artefacts render the potential unusable for high-pressure, radiation-damage, or shock simulations, and are conventionally patched by splicing in an empirical screened-Coulomb baseline such as ZBL \todo{cite: ZBL 1985; examples of ZBL splicing in MLIP literature}, at the cost of introducing an ad hoc, element-pair-specific reference potential and a crossover region that must be tuned by hand.

% --- Paragraph 4: our approach. ---
In this work we take a different route: rather than adding data or empirical baselines, we impose physical knowledge directly on the model parameters at training time. For linear MLIPs the least-squares fit can be recast as a quadratic program (QP), and physical requirements---repulsive cores, curvature conditions, or constraints derived from experimentally measured properties---enter naturally as linear constraints on the coefficients. The resulting fit remains convex and is solved to global optimality by standard QP solvers \todo{cite: OSQP or whichever solver you used; QP textbook e.g. Boyd \& Vandenberghe}.

% --- Paragraph 5: contributions, mirrors your "Main points" list. ---
Our contributions are as follows. First, we show that physics-informed linear constraints patch holes in the PES at negligible computational cost, providing an alternative to DFT dataset expansion (Section~\ref{sec:results_pareto}). Second, we demonstrate constraints formulated from experimental data, sidestepping poorly converged DFT stresses for elastic and vibrational properties (Section~\ref{sec:results_experimental}). Third, we show that constrained models recover strictly repulsive cores, giving access to high-pressure conditions without ZBL splicing (Section~\ref{sec:results_core}). Finally, we integrate uncertainty quantification via POPS committees and analyse how the constraints alter uncertainty estimates in and out of the training distribution (Section~\ref{sec:results_uq}).

\subsection{Constrained fitting as quadratic programming}
\label{sec:methods_qp}
Enforcing linear constraints during training is achieved within the quadratic programming framework:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    & \text{minimize} \quad \tfrac{1}{2}\,c^{T} P c + q^{T}c \\
    & \text{subject to} \quad l \leq Ac \leq u,
\end{split}
\label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
where $c \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ are the model parameters, $P \in \mathbb{S}_{+}^{d}$ is the regularised Gram matrix $P := B^{T}B + \Gamma^{T}\Gamma$, and $q := -B^{T}y$ \todo{sign check: the QP objective equivalent to least squares needs $q = -B^Ty$}. The constraint matrix $A$, together with lower and upper bounds $l, u$, encodes the physical priors described below.
